The through-line

Why these fit together.

Potential is everything a technology could do. Value is the part of that potential an organization actually captures, and getting the two working in concert is the real trick. Potential shows up with no clear map for adoption or an authentic picture of how people behave or the environments they work in, so most of it goes unrealized. Closing that distance is a human problem: engaging with what the engineers built, making sense of what they actually intended, and mapping both onto the outcomes the people inside the business are already trying to reach, in terms they can understand and act on.

The industry's dominant weakness is really its core strength over-extended. It is an engineering culture and worldview, pushed past its natural boundaries without a counterweight, and it shows up as the familiar habit of building a capability, then going looking for the problem it solves, then assuming those with that problem see the value in the solution the same way as those who built the technology. That cycle results in impressive demos, slick slide decks, but stalled pilots and a finance team that cannot find the return on the investment. The correction is not more engineering. It is to make sure engineering does not walk that road alone, but alongside someone who can weigh, from the side of the person and the business, what is actually worth building, and why, in terms that get the support and funding necessary to achieve the desired outcome.

I have spent a career addressing this challenge, from communications and influence, to business development and sales, to change and adoption. Always technology focused, in multiple industries, from retail and consumer goods to B2B tech, from semiconductors and internet infrastructure to telecommunications and manufacturing. For the last two years I have found new inspiration in the exciting emergence of applied AI solutions, moving from strategy to fingers on keyboards to gain first-hand understanding of what these new tools and platforms can actually do to help improve the work people do and the outcomes they can produce.
