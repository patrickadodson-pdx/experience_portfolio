AI Factory Stack · Exposure & perspective

Technical capacity gets procured. Business outcomes stall.

The industry is pouring capital into AI factories: GPU clusters, high-performance networking, AI-optimized storage, and the orchestration that runs them. The engineering is real and the spend is enormous. The value keeps stalling one layer above the hardware, where a built capability has to become applied use a business adopts and finance can measure.

That layer is the one I have worked in my whole career, across enterprise hardware, semiconductor manufacturing, venture-backed startups, and internet governance. The engineers understand the factory; my work has always been to get the honest version of what it is for out of them and carry it to a business that has learned to distrust the hype, or grown fatigued by experiments that never produce a personal or business benefit. I have not sold or built GPU, networking, or storage infrastructure. What I bring sits one level up, and that is exactly where this spend keeps stalling and putting the investment beneath it at risk.

The terrain, described neutrally

The factory, by layer.

This is the stack as the infrastructure market frames it, read from the physical layer up to the applied one. I am describing what each layer does and who the named players are as a sense-making exercise, not a build recommendation. The integration model that assembles it is architecture-led and multi-OEM: validated designs that combine components from several vendors into one system, rather than a single-vendor appliance.

How the market sells it

The prevailing model is architecture-led and multi-OEM: an integrator validates a reference design that combines compute, networking, and storage from several vendors into one supported system. The value proposition is the integration and the validation, not any single component.

Where the spend concentrates

Capital and attention cluster at L1 and L2, the facility and the accelerators, because that is where the scarcity and the headlines are. The layers that turn capacity into a usable, governed platform get less deliberate attention, and the applied layer above them gets the least.

Where the industry missteps

The infrastructure-to-applied gap.

The stack above is the market's map, described as I find it. What follows is my perspective on the state of play in this environment.

Watch where the money goes and where the attention follows, and the shape is unmistakable. The capital pours into the bottom two layers, the facility and the accelerators, because that is where the scarcity is. That is the investment, not the value. The value is claimed at the top layer, the applied outcome, in board decks and press releases. In between sits the gap where the value is actually created, and no amount of additional compute or press release closes it, because it was never a compute or a PR problem.

The buyer procures the factory and assumes the outcome will follow. It does not. The same thing happens here that happened with the data platform one layer up: the capability gets built, and nobody translated it into applied use a business adopts and finance can measure.

None of that capacity produces a business result on its own, no matter how much of it a company buys. The result shows up only when people work differently because of what the factory makes possible, and getting there is a human problem the hardware budget never touches. More networking will not move it. More storage will not move it. What moves it is adoption, governance, and honest measurement, and those are practices a business runs, not components an integrator ships.

That translation, from built capability to an outcome the C-suite recognizes, is where my skillset is best deployed. It is the layer above the infrastructure, where a career spent getting organizations to actually change how they work meets two years of building on these tools closely enough to separate what is real and useful from what is merely sold or shown on a slide.

Where you are

From procured to adopted.

A readiness ladder for the same terrain, framed by what is actually true of the outcome rather than what was installed. Most reported spend clusters on the earliest rungs; most claimed value is asserted at the final one; the deliberate work happens on the middle rungs, which stay ambiguous and under-addressed.

Figure 2 · AI factory readiness, capacity procured to business adopted
1
Procured

The capacity is bought or reserved. Compute, networking, and storage exist on the balance sheet and in the data center. The narrative is about scale.

To advance: a running platform, where the cluster is orchestrated, utilized, and shared rather than sitting idle.
2
Operational

The factory runs. Jobs are scheduled, utilization is managed, models train and serve. The infrastructure team can point to a working platform.

To advance: real workloads tied to a business use, not benchmarks and internal experiments.
3
Applied

Production use cases run on the platform and touch real work. Something a team does is now done with AI in the loop rather than in a pilot.

To advance: the rung the market skips, where the people whose work it touches actually adopt it as the default way of working.
4
Adopted

The new way is the default rather than the exception. The workforce changed how it works, the old workaround is gone, and the change held past the launch.

To advance: the measurement that lets finance connect the adoption to a number, not an anecdote.
5
Measurably improving the business

The outcome shows up where a CFO looks: cost, cycle time, revenue, risk. The factory stops being a capacity story and becomes the engine that drives business results, with a clear line back to the spend.

The frontier that matters: almost all reported investment sits at rungs one and two, the value is claimed at rung five, and rungs three and four, the adoption work, are where the gap actually lives.

This ladder describes the path to business value, not the infrastructure underneath it. It is the frame I would use with a business audience to locate where a stalled AI investment is actually stuck, which is almost never the layer getting the most budget.
